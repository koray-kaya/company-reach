# Partner mode: recruiting two or three design-partner firms

Desk research for issue #84, 2026-10-03. Gathered by an AI research agent and
spot-checked by hand (see "How far to trust this" at the end). **[E]** has a
source; **[O]** is opinion or inference. Most numbers come from other
countries and other kinds of study: read them as ranges, not forecasts.

## The question

The survey campaign asks many firms for ten minutes. The partner mode asks a
few firms for much more: three or four rounds of long interviews and mockup
feedback over several months. What does it take to get two or three firms of
exactly the target type to say yes, and what does that change in the tool?

## 1. What a design partnership usually asks

- **Cadence [E]:** in Common Paper's agreement data, 46% of agreements with
  regular feedback set two sessions a month and 36% one a month; 49% run for
  three or six months
  ([Common Paper](https://commonpaper.com/standards/design-partner-agreement/)).
  Unusual Ventures recommends 30–45-minute calls every two weeks
  ([guide](https://www.unusual.vc/field-guide/module-4-working-with-design-partners/));
  Sierra held weekly 30-minute meetings for two to six months
  ([First Round](https://review.firstround.com/sierra-design-partnership/)).
- **[O]** That is about 4–12 hours per partner. "A few hours" is lighter than
  usual, and the mail should say so in numbers.
- **What partners get [E]:** a product shaped to their needs, extra attention,
  free or cheaper access
  ([Common Paper blog](https://commonpaper.com/blog/design-partner/)).
- **Why firms say no [E]:** among German firms with 20+ staff that do not work
  with startups: no time 47%, no money 44%, no contacts 42%, no suitable
  project 34%, no added value 30%
  ([Bitkom 2022, n=604](https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Unternehmen-Kooperation-Startups-keine-Zeit-Geld-Kontakte)).
  They say yes when the pain is acute and they have already tried to fix it
  themselves (Unusual guide).

## 2. Documented funnels

- **Startups [E, two cases]:** Sierra listed 40 candidates ("some friends,
  some strangers") to land 4 and ended with 6; Traceable aimed for 50+
  discovery conversations in a quarter and landed 5
  ([Unusual](https://www.unusual.vc/articles/how-traceable-found-design-partners)).
- **Small workplaces, warm against cold [E]:** Hammerback et al. 2018
  recruited 78 small low-wage worksites in the United States for a health
  programme. *Of the eligible worksites*, 32% of cold ones (a purchased list),
  48% of lukewarm ones (a partner organisation's list) and 60% of warm ones
  (personal referral) enrolled, and cold worksites were twice as likely to be
  ineligible ([abstract](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/76452)). The agent
  also reported shares of *all* worksites contacted (5%, 13%, 29%) and
  meeting rates (9%, 18%, 46%) from the paper's tables; those were not
  re-checked. The team phoned up to 15 times per worksite, so mail alone will
  do worse.
- **Field access [E]:** 4 of 29 organisations approached granted access for
  fieldwork
  ([Kemp 2010](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/17537981011089587/full/html)).
- **Dropout [E]:** a five-year study kept 36 of 97 small businesses; the firms
  that stayed were larger, and leaders' time was the main barrier
  ([Small+Safe+Well](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9128251)).
  Dropout across three or four short rounds is not documented.

## 3. Cold mail for a large ask

- **Rates [E]:** sales benchmarks disagree and count any reply to a sales
  mail: 7% in 2023
  ([Belkins 2023](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-outreach-statistics)),
  0.45% over 7.5 million mails in 2026, with 58.6% of replies coming from
  follow-ups
  ([Belkins 2026](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics)).
  Surveys of executives average 32%, raised only by a topic that matters to
  them and backing from someone in their network
  ([Cycyota & Harrison 2006](https://doi.org/10.1177/1094428105280770));
  surveys addressed to organisations average 35.7%
  ([Baruch & Holtom 2008](https://doi.org/10.1177/0018726708094863)). The
  estimates in `LEARNINGS.md` §5 (8–15% for a named address, 2–6% for
  `info@`) are for the ten-minute survey and still unmeasured.
- **University as sender [E]:** raised response to postal questionnaires
  (odds ratio 1.32) but not to email ones (0.84, interval 0.69–1.01);
  personalised email did help (1.24)
  ([Cochrane review 2023](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/MR000008_how-can-response-postal-or-web-questionnaires-be-increased)).
  **[O]** The school's name makes the request credible; on its own it will not
  get meetings.
- **Time against value [E]:** for small-business owners a longer task lowered
  willingness more than incentives made up for
  ([Seshadri & Broekemier 2022](https://doi.org/10.53703/001c.32575)).
- **A small first ask [E, O]:** the foot-in-the-door effect holds only under
  some conditions
  ([Burger 1999](https://cbsm.com/articles/32623-the-foot-in-the-door-compliance-procedure-a-multiple-process-analysis-and-review)),
  but every recruitment above went in two steps: a meeting first, the
  commitment after it.

## 4. Who to write to

**[E]** Whether an organisation answers depends on whether the person asked
has the authority, the capacity and a reason to answer
([Tomaskovic-Devey et al. 1994](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Donald-Tomaskovic-Devey/publication/271760918_Organizational_Survey_Nonresponse/links/5745893b08ae9f741b41ca43/Organizational-Survey-Nonresponse.pdf)).
**[O]** Write to the owner or managing director by name; in a firm above about
30 staff, ask them to name the head of sales or purchasing.

## 6. Legal and ethics for a long partnership

- **Recording [E]:** recording a non-public conversation without every
  participant's consent is a criminal offence
  ([StGB Art. 179ter](https://www.droit-bilingue.ch/en-fr/3/31/311.0-179ter-221.html)):
  written consent from each participant, asked again aloud when each
  recording starts.
- **Information [E]:** people are told who processes their data, for what,
  who receives it and whether it goes abroad
  ([FADP Art. 19](https://prighter.com/resources/laws/swiss-fadp/fadp/articles/article-19));
  research data is anonymised as soon as the purpose allows
  ([Art. 31(2)(e)](https://prighter.com/resources/laws/swiss-fadp/fadp/articles/article-31)).
  **[O]** Name the video, transcription and language-model tools used.
- **Firm data [O]:** firms will worry more about business secrets than about
  personal data. A one-page confidentiality commitment signed by the student
  and the supervisor; the school checks any NDA a firm sends. A one-page,
  non-binding letter of intent: the rounds, the hours, the contact people,
  what the firm gets, the right to stop at any time. Do not copy the startup
  clause that the provider owns all feedback
  ([E: Common Paper](https://commonpaper.com/standards/design-partner-agreement/)).
- **Review [E]:** the Human Research Act covers health research only
  ([University of Fribourg](https://www.unifr.ch/research/en/ethics/human-research/overview.html));
  whether the school wants its own review is a question for the school.

## 7. What this means for the partner mode [O]

1. **Two steps.** The mail asks for a 30-minute first conversation (phone,
   video or a visit) and states the whole plan in numbers, for example three
   sessions of 60–90 minutes until spring 2027, about 4–6 hours, stop at any
   time. The partnership is agreed only after a good first conversation.
2. **Plan the funnel.** Keep 2–3 partners → get 4 to commit → hold 8–12 first
   conversations → reach them through roughly 20–25 warm introductions, or
   100–300 checked cold mails to named owners, or a mix such as 10–15 warm
   plus 60–120 cold. These are rough: their basis is section 2, and mail does
   worse than phone.
3. **A stricter fit than the survey's.** Makes or processes a product, buys
   from suppliers, sells to businesses, 10–49 staff (up to 99), a named
   decision-maker, and within reach for a visit if sessions are face to face.
4. **The owner or managing director, by name.** A firm with only `info@` goes
   to a phone call or a manual check, not to a mail.
5. **A different mail.** About 150 words, formal *Sie*: greeting by name; who
   writes, with the supervisor; one sentence on what this firm makes, buys
   and sells; the problem in the firm's words; the ask and the plan; what the
   firm gets; not a sales call and no product name; confidentiality and the
   data lines. Reviewed in Turkish first, then written as natural German.
6. **A warm path.** The tool could also draft a short introduction request
   for supervisors and contacts, and a mail variant that names who referred.
7. **The follow-up rule.** The survey mail promises "Ich schreibe Ihnen nur
   dieses eine Mal" for good. Whether the partner mode may follow up once, or
   phone, is for the supervisors to decide; follow-ups carry a large share of
   replies in vendor data.
8. **Measure.** Log each firm's channel (warm, lukewarm, cold) and stage
   (written, replied, first conversation, committed, partner), so real rates
   replace the guesses above.
9. **Keep the survey's promise.** Every firm that got the survey mail was told
   "Ich schreibe Ihnen nur dieses eine Mal". The partner mode skips those
   firms, except respondents who asked to be contacted at the end of the
   survey: they are the warmest leads there are.
10. **Keep the tool general.** What the mail asks (the first conversation, the
    sessions, the hours) belongs in the profile, as the survey's minutes do
    today, not in code written for one thesis.

## Open questions for when the work starts

- Face-to-face or online sessions? The answer sets the region to pool.
- What does a firm get in return, for example a short scan of its possible
  customers, suppliers or competitors?
- May the partner mode follow up once, or phone?
- The survey code stays. Is the survey still sent alongside?
- Does the school want its own review before the first conversation?
